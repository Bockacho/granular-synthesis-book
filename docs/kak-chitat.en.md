# How to read this book

This book is written for two readers at once. The main text is for someone who has never opened a granular synthesizer. The "Under the hood" boxes explain the physics, the formulas and how the algorithms work. You can skip the boxes without losing the thread of the story.

The book goes from simple to complex. [Part 1](chast-1-zerno/index.md) explains what a sound grain is. [Part 2](chast-2-parametry/index.md) takes each parameter apart, one at a time, in detail. [Part 3](chast-3-instrumenty/index.md) walks through everything Serum 2, Granulator III and Pigments can do. [Part 4](chast-4-praktika/index.md) is for interesting combinations, and [Part 5](chast-5-dalshe-sam/index.md) holds the exercises, the glossary and the free plugins.

## A sound to practice on

Before the first chapter, record the clink of a teaspoon against a cup on your phone. It has a sharp knock, a clear ring and a quiet, drawn-out tail, so every granulator parameter will sound different on it.

How to record it: a quiet room, the phone 20–30 cm from the cup, one hit, and let the ring fade out on its own. Move the file to your computer and trim the silence at the start. Leave the silence at the end, you will need it in [chapter 4](chast-2-parametry/04-istochnik-i-poziciya.md).

## What you will need

Any DAW: Ableton Live, FL Studio, Logic, Reaper, Bitwig. One of the three instruments from [Part 3](chast-3-instrumenty/index.md). Headphones or monitors: many grain effects live in stereo and vanish on laptop speakers. If you don't have an instrument yet, check the manufacturer's site for a trial version.

Every chapter ends with an exercise of 5–15 minutes. Granular synthesis is learned by ear, and it's hard to grasp by reading alone.
